Research robot · In development

Meet Alp.

A research robot designed to make human-robot interaction easier to build and explore.

Alp brings expressive digital interaction into a physical research platform, uniting an approachable design with software built for experimentation.

Explore the designDiscuss a research pilot

Designed for interaction

A familiar presence.
A flexible research tool.

A rounded face, a slim enclosure, and a clear touchscreen give the intended robot an approachable physical presence. The design centers on the ways people communicate: looking, listening, speaking, and choosing.

Camera and audio integration, an expressive display, and custom screen content are being developed as parts of one system. Researchers could shape those elements around a study, a classroom activity, or a new interaction idea.

Affordability, serviceability, and accessible development guide our engineering choices. Pricing and availability have not been announced.

A physical point of connection

More ways to ask a question.

Spoken dialogue, visual feedback, and touchscreen choices open different ways to study an interaction. Alp is designed to bring them together in a consistent physical setting.

Imagine exploring how a display cue changes a conversation, pairing a photo prompt with spoken questions, or building a guided classroom activity. These are intended research uses; the integrated interaction system is still in development.

Planned first-generation capability

A mobile base, thoughtfully integrated.

Indoor mobility is part of Alp's intended first-generation design. A discreet wheeled base would expand the settings researchers could explore while keeping interaction at the center.

Mobility has not been demonstrated. Base integration and movement control require development and validation before they can support research applications.

Where we are today

Building the system behind the design.

Current work connects the robot's physical head, sensing and audio, expressive interfaces, and application software. Early prototypes demonstrate camera and audio capture, robot-side application execution, and local application deployment foundations.

The finished enclosure, integrated interaction experience, broader developer interfaces, and mobile base remain in development. The product concepts show the intended direction of the complete robot.

Our target is a first research-ready pilot robot by the end of the 2026–27 academic year (spring 2027), subject to development and validation. This is a development milestone, not a guaranteed launch or shipping date.

Application spotlight · Research

A conversation can begin with a memory.

A photo-guided reminiscence research application exploring spoken, personalized interaction with people living with dementia.

Inspired by an earlier Pepper research prototype. Adaptation for the HumanFrame platform is in development.

The experience

Photos create a place to start.

A familiar image can give a conversation a starting point. The application explores how a robot might listen and respond through spoken dialogue, supported by on-screen content and expressive feedback.

The earlier Pepper prototype, developed by Adrian in a RoboCare Lab setting, connected a tablet-based interface, photo selection, spoken dialogue, and an AI-backed conversation loop. That prior implementation informs the HumanFrame adaptation; it does not demonstrate a completed application on Alp.

Prior research workflow

From a visual prompt to an open conversation.

The completed Pepper prototype brought these parts of the interaction together. Their adaptation to HumanFrame remains in progress.

  1. Choose a memory prompt

    After a simple initial login, the tablet presented a grid of nine photos. Selecting an image provided a starting point for recollection.

  2. Start a guided conversation

    The chosen image gave context to AI-generated questions and responses, intended to invite open-ended reminiscence rather than test someone for correct answers.

  3. Continue through voice and expression

    The prototype linked audio capture, transcription, conversational responses, synthesized speech, tablet feedback, and optional robot behavior in a continuing interaction.

Research purpose

A tangible setting for studying conversational robotics.

The project brings human-centered interface design, conversational AI, and robot embodiment together to explore engagement in dementia-care research. It offers concrete questions for future investigation:

  • How can familiar visual prompts make it easier to begin an interaction?
  • How does a robot's physical presence influence a conversation?
  • How can speech, on-screen content, and expressive feedback work together?
  • How might a reusable framework make similar applications easier to build?

This is research exploring conversational engagement. It is not a proven therapy, diagnostic tool, dementia treatment, or replacement for clinical care.

The HumanFrame foundation

One application. A reusable foundation.

HumanFrame's framework is being designed to connect custom experiences with supported robot capabilities through a consistent software layer. Reminiscence is one example of the research applications that could be built and adapted on that foundation.
